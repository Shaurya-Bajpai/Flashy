Privacy Policy

Effective date: July 12, 2026  ·  Last updated: July 12, 2026

1. Overview

Plain-language summary: Flashy blinks your camera flash for calls, messages, and app notifications. It reads notification titles and phone call state on-device only — this data never leaves your phone. The only external data sharing is anonymous crash and usage statistics sent to Google Firebase, which you can opt out of in your Google account settings.

This Privacy Policy explains what information the Flashy application accesses, how it is used, and your rights. By installing or using Flashy, you agree to the terms described here.

Flashy is developed by Nickelfox Technologies Pvt. Ltd. If you have questions, contact us at aanchal.negi@nickelfox.com.

2. What We Do and Don't Do With Your Data

We Do

  • Read notification titles to detect who sent a message
  • Detect when your phone is ringing
  • Store your app settings locally on your device
  • Keep a local flash history log (last 30 events)
  • Sample microphone volume in real time (opt-in only)
  • Send anonymous crash reports to Firebase Crashlytics
  • Send anonymous usage statistics to Firebase Analytics

We Don't

  • Record, store, or upload audio from your microphone
  • Read the content of your messages or notifications
  • Access your contacts, call logs, or phone numbers
  • Upload notification titles or sender names to any server
  • Track your location at any time
  • Sell, rent, or share personal data with third parties
  • Show ads or use data for targeted advertising
  • Create user accounts or require registration
  • Sync your settings to the cloud

3. Permissions Explained

Below is every Android permission Flashy requests, why it is needed, and how the data is used.


CAMERA Required
Controls the rear flash LED (torch mode). Flashy never opens the camera, captures photos, or records video. It only calls CameraManager.setTorchMode() to turn the flash on or off.
BIND_NOTIFICATION_LISTENER_SERVICE Required
Allows Flashy to receive notification events so it can trigger a flash when a message or app notification arrives. Flashy reads only the notification title (sender's display name or app name) — it does not read the notification body, message content, images, or any other data.
This permission requires explicit user approval in Android Settings → Notification Access. It cannot be granted automatically.
READ_PHONE_STATE Required for call flash
Detects when the phone transitions to the RINGING state so the flash can blink before a call is answered. Flashy does not read phone numbers, IMEI, subscriber ID, or any other device identifier. It only observes the call state (idle, ringing, off-hook).
RECORD_AUDIO Optional — Sound Reactive mode only
Only requested if you enable Sound Reactive Flash in Settings. When active, Flashy samples the microphone approximately every 100ms to measure the ambient sound level (RMS amplitude). The raw audio buffer is discarded immediately after the amplitude is calculated — it is never written to storage or transmitted. When you disable Sound Reactive Flash, microphone sampling stops immediately.
You will see a full disclosure dialog before this permission is requested. You can deny it; the flash alert features work without it.
FOREGROUND_SERVICE Auto — System
Required to run the flash alert service in the foreground (with a visible notification). This ensures the service is not killed when the screen turns off. The persistent notification reads "Flash Alerts Active" and is kept at low importance so it does not interrupt you.
FOREGROUND_SERVICE_CAMERA Auto — System
Required on Android 10+ when a foreground service accesses the camera hardware (flash LED). Declared so Android knows this service uses camera, not for photography — just torch mode.
FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MICROPHONE Conditional — Sound Reactive only
Required on Android 12+ when a foreground service accesses the microphone. Flashy's service only activates this type at runtime when both Sound Reactive Flash is enabled AND the RECORD_AUDIO permission is granted. It is never activated otherwise.
POST_NOTIFICATIONS Required on Android 13+
Required by Android 13 (API 33) and above to display the foreground service notification ("Flash Alerts Active"). Without this, the service cannot run reliably in the foreground on Android 13+ devices.
RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED Auto — System
Allows Flashy to restart the flash alert service automatically after the device reboots. Without this, flash alerts would stop working every time the phone is restarted until the user manually opens the app.
WAKE_LOCK Auto — System
Keeps the CPU briefly active while a flash sequence is running to ensure the LED blinks the correct number of times. Without this, budget devices from Xiaomi, Oppo, Realme, and Samsung may cut the flash sequence short when the screen is off. Wake locks are released immediately after each flash sequence ends.
REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS Optional — Prompted during setup
Requests exemption from Android's battery optimization (Doze mode) so the flash alert service is not suspended when the phone is idle. Without this, the flash may fail on aggressive power-saving OEM builds (Xiaomi MIUI, Oppo ColorOS, Realme UI, Samsung One UI). The user is prompted during onboarding and can decline; the app functions without it on stock Android.

5. Notification Listener — How It Works

Flashy uses Android's NotificationListenerService API to receive notification events. This is a sensitive Android API that requires explicit user approval in Settings → Notification Access.

When a notification arrives, Flashy reads only the title field (Notification.EXTRA_TITLE) — this is the sender's name as shown at the top of the notification, for example "Mom" or "WhatsApp". It does not read:

  • The message body or text content
  • Notification images or rich media
  • Reply-to or contact phone numbers
  • Conversation history
  • Any data from ongoing (persistent) notifications

The title is used for two purposes only: (1) checking whether the sender matches a contact filter you configured, and (2) adding an entry to the local flash history log. The title is never uploaded, shared, or stored outside the device.

6. Third-Party Services — Firebase

Flashy uses two Firebase services operated by Google LLC:

Firebase Crashlytics — Automatically collects crash reports to help us identify and fix bugs. A report includes the exception type, stack trace, device model, Android OS version, and app version. It does not include notification content, audio data, contact names from your filters, or any message text.

Firebase Analytics — Collects anonymous usage data: app open/close events, which flash features are enabled, general device category (phone/tablet), and country/region (coarse). It does not collect precise location, notification content, or personally identifiable information.

Both services are governed by Google's Privacy Policy: policies.google.com/privacy. You can opt out of Analytics personalization through your Google account settings at myaccount.google.com.

7. Data Retention and Deletion

All app data (settings, contact filters, per-app rules, flash history) is stored exclusively on your device using Android DataStore. This data is retained until one of the following:

  • You clear the flash history from the app dashboard (clears history only)
  • You uninstall Flashy (removes all app data)
  • You clear the app's storage in Android Settings → Apps → Flashy → Clear Storage

Flash history is automatically limited to the 30 most recent events — older entries are discarded automatically.

Firebase Crashlytics and Analytics data is retained on Google's servers per Google's own retention policies (typically 90 days for crash reports).

9. Your Rights

Because Flashy stores all personal data locally on your device, you have full control:

  • Access: All your data is visible in the app (settings, contact filters, flash history)
  • Delete: Clear flash history from the dashboard; uninstall the app to delete everything
  • Withdraw consent: Disable Notification Access or any permission at any time in Android Settings
  • Opt out of analytics: Via your Google account privacy settings

If you are in the European Economic Area (EEA), you have additional rights under GDPR. Since we do not directly collect or process personal data on our servers, your rights requests are fulfilled by managing the app and your device directly. For Firebase-related requests, contact Google at their privacy request form.

10. Security

Flashy implements the following security measures:

  • All local data is stored in Android DataStore with allowBackup="false" to prevent ADB backup extraction
  • Cloud backup and device-transfer backup are explicitly disabled for the DataStore via data_extraction_rules.xml
  • The flash service is not exported (android:exported="false") — it cannot be started by other apps
  • Debug logging is stripped in release builds using ProGuard
  • No network requests are made by Flashy's own code — only Firebase SDKs use the network
